# 1. Why This Article

A lot is being written and said about burnout but I believe most takes are trying to impose a single explanation (and solution) onto all cases of burnout. I believe there is a lot more nuance to the problem and that there is probably just as much variation in the causes, effects, and solutions of burnout as there is in the lives, personalities, and motivations of software engineers.

## A Wealthy Man !== An All Knowing Man
I don't know why people do this. If a man is wealthy (financially successful), they give a lot more weight to everything (s)he says even when (s)he speaks about matters that are clearly way outside their domain of expertise. Why would you treat Elon Musk as an expert on history?

So when you find financially successful oversimplifying the problem to a version that suits their worldview, people unfortunately start listening. Here's Sam Altman about what he thinks is the cause of burnout:

<SamAltmanBurnoutYouTubeVideo id="qnRj_fYlAiA" title="Sam Altman on burnout" />

Sam says *"I find that I have infinite energy to work on things that I find interesting"* and takes that to mean that all people must feel the same. *My experience equals everyone's experience.* I don't think I need to convince you that that, of course, cannot be the case.

For some people, *"working on things that aren't interesting to them or aren't working"* may actually be the primary cause of their burnout. For others it may be a secondary/minor cause or not a cause at all.

## The Lesson from this?
Listen to all opinions but take each with a pinch of salt. Just because someone claims to be an "expert" on burnout or mental health, does not mean you should take their word for it.
This holds true for my article as well. I may be right, I may be wrong. So hear me out and decide for yourself.

# 3. What causes burnout?
No single thing. But mentioned below is a list of possible causes (not an exhaustive list) and their possible solutions. Pick the ones that seem relatable and think/read more about it. This may help you identify the primary, secondary, and other causes (yes, don't assume there is just oen cause).

## a. Anxiety Disorder
Your body constantly staying in a state of anxiety burns life fuel. This is fuel you use when you want to exert willpower, pick up a task, wake up early, handle meetings, solve problems, write code, etc. If your mind and body are burning this fuel all the time while merely existing, eventually you'll run out of this fuel and it won't be because you worked too hard. 

### Possible Solution
First determine that this is in fact the primary cause. One way to do it is to go to a psychiatrist and get medication for anxiety (the kind that provides quick short term relief). If it works and after around a week of use you feel yourself reinvigorated, then you now know that anxiety was a major cause.

Once sure, you can then switch to long-term safer medications for medication, get therapy, meditate, exercise, and address your anxiety. Don't work on fixing your burnout. Work on fixing its cause.

## b. Depression
Anxiety and depression often go hand-in-hand (but not always). Depression may leave you feeling powerless. 

### Possible Solution
Adopt a similar solution to the one described above for anxiety. It may also be possible to identify a cause for your depression (there isn't always one but sometimes there is) and address that. That way you fix the cause of the cause, the root of the issue. Maybe you need a better social life, sex, or something else.

## c. Seasonal Affective Disorder
This may be a contributing factor if not the primary cause. 
If you notice that you felt burnt out in winters, that may not be a coincidence. In winters, reduced sunlight leads to lower serotonin levels and a disruption of the sleep-wake cycle. This results in depressed mood, low energy, and fatigue, making the normal stresses of work feel heavier and accelerating the feeling of emotional exhaustion characteristic of burnout.

### Possible Solutions
Possible solutions involve Light Therapy (using a SAD lamp) to simulate sunlight and regulate mood/sleep cycles, and implementing self-care strategies like scheduling exercise, prioritizing sleep hygiene, and actively managing workload to reduce cumulative stress.

## d. Lifestyle
Lifestyle is a broad word but what I specifically mean by this is things like:
1. The kind of food you are eating
2. The quality and timing of your sleep
3. Lack of movement and physical exercise
4. Remote work

- [Studies](https://www.healthline.com/health/fast-food-effects-on-body#:~:text=Research%20from%202018%20also%20suggested,they%20were%2017%20years%20old.) consistently show that a diet high in fast food (characterized by refined sugars, unhealthy fats, and processed ingredients) is associated with an increased risk of developing depression, heightened stress, and anxiety symptoms.
- Insufficient sleep and poor quality sleep (e.g., fragmented sleep) are strongly and bidirectionally linked to an increased risk and severity of depression and anxiety. ([Sources](https://www.psychiatry.org/news-room/apa-blogs/timing-of-sleep-can-be-important#:~:text=The%20amount%20and%20quality%20of,anxiety%2C%20bipolar%20disorder%20and%20schizophrenia.))
- Physical inactivity is directly linked to higher rates of depression and anxiety, as regular movement releases endorphins and serotonin (mood-boosting chemicals) and helps to lower stress hormones like cortisol. ([Sources](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317372264_Mental_health_consequences_of_exercise_withdrawal_A_systematic_review#:~:text=In%20addition%2C%20physical%20activity%2Dbased,increased%20depressive%20symptoms%20(6)%20.))
- The primary mental health risks from extended remote work stem from a lack of boundaries and a lack of social connection.

## e. Lack of Social Life
Lack of social connection heightens health risks as much as smoking 15 cigarettes a day or having alcohol use disorder. Perceived social isolation is linked to an increased risk of developing depression and anxiety. Strong social ties act as a vital stress buffer.

## f. Work Misfit
You may be working at a company where the work doesn't align with your identity, purpose, goals, etc. Maybe you have a bad manager or tough peers to work with. Maybe you're more suited to a late-stage company but you're working at an early stage company or vice versa. Maybe the company and work are perfect but you don't have friends at work (that helps tremendously).

# 4. Conclusion: What to do
No one can tell you what you should do, but I guess you would do what one does with any disease. First find out what's the root cause of the issue (though I'm not even convinced there always is an identifiable root cause, but assuming there hopefully usually is). Then once again ask if this is the base cause or is there a cause for this cause. Once you have the base cause, treat it. Once treated, you may need further rest still to recover from the damage already done. The burnout should go away and not come back.
