**TL;DR:** If you export freelance computer or information services, make sure your payment comes with documentation supporting purpose code **9186**. A supported USD wire can help. A PKR payout can work too, if the provider and receiving institution explicitly support the correct code.

Getting paid in dollars is one thing. Getting the paperwork right when that money reaches Pakistan is another.

You can receive the money, spend it, and later discover that your bank recorded your software work as a home remittance. This post is about avoiding that headache.

## What is a PRC, and why 9186?

A proceeds realization certificate, or PRC, documents money received from abroad. Its purpose code tells you how the payment was classified.

SBP assigns 9186 to freelance computer and information services. Code 9471 covers money sent home for family maintenance by Pakistani workers living abroad. Those are different transactions. If you write software in Pakistan for overseas clients, your records should reflect the services you actually provided. [SBP's purpose-code list](https://www.sbp.org.pk/fe_returns/cod5.pdf) explains both categories.

**9186 is not the universal code for every freelancer or every software engineer's salary.** Your contract and the nature of your income matter. An actual employee, including someone employed locally through an employer-of-record arrangement, should confirm the appropriate treatment with their bank and adviser.

A wrong code can leave you explaining why your invoices and bank records do not match. I'd sort that out early. I would not claim that 9471 automatically triggers an FBR audit, or that having 9186 takes care of your tax obligations.

## When a company pays you through Deel or Rippling

A US company may use Deel or Rippling to handle contractor payments. The company funds the platform, and you configure how to receive the money through its website or app. That takes the payment administration off the company's hands.

Your options depend on the product and account. The distinction to look for is a **local PKR payout** versus a **USD international transfer**. Rippling describes support for [PKR or foreign-currency contractor payments](https://www.rippling.com/country-hiring/pakistan-contractors), while Deel lets contractors choose their [recipient currency and payout method](https://help.letsdeel.com/hc/en-gb/articles/26554806456593-How-to-Use-Deel-Wallet).

This choice can be hidden in plain sight. In the Rippling flow I used, the bank-account-currency field looked like it was asking about my Pakistani account. In practice, what mattered was the currency and route used to send the payment.

Choosing USD also revealed an interbank-routing toggle. **Keep that disabled** for the direct USD route I'm describing.

**The two Rippling settings I would check**

- Transfer currency: **USD**
- Interbank routing: **Disabled**

That's the interface I encountered. Labels and available options can change. Do not falsely describe a PKR account as a USD account. Ask support whether the field refers to the account's denomination or the payout currency. Also check that your bank can accept a USD wire into your particular account and convert it to PKR.

## What happens with a local PKR payout?

Suppose a US company owes you $2,000 and you want it in your Meezan account. The provider can arrange a local payout through a Pakistani partner. Your account receives rupees through IBFT, an interbank funds transfer, while the international settlement happens elsewhere in the chain.

Company → payment platform / Pakistani payout partner → PKR via IBFT → your Meezan account

*Simplified payout path. The provider's international settlement is separate.*

The partner-bank examples from my experience and understanding are Askari for Rippling and Bank Alfalah for Deel. Treat these as examples, not permanent routing commitments. Ask the provider which institution handled your actual transfer.

**The advantage is speed.** A local transfer can arrive quickly. The downside is that your bank may see the local credit without the foreign-payment details it needs to document your freelance income correctly.

I wouldn't assume the provider first sends your exact dollars into its own Pakistani account and then pays you while those dollars are processing. It may use prefunded balances or other settlement arrangements. What matters to you is the information accompanying the payout.

If that route classifies your earnings as a home remittance, you can end up with 9471. Correcting it may require the provider and intermediary bank to supply or amend the original payment information.

In the interbank case I encountered, **the receiving bank issued the PRC, but said it could not choose or change the purpose code** because it came from the upstream payment information. That's the frustrating part. The bank handing you the certificate may depend on another institution to correct the data behind it.

Your receiving bank still has a role. SBP's different-bank ePRC model says the beneficiary bank issues the certificate using complete transaction information supplied by the intermediary. Ask who holds that information and who needs to correct it, instead of getting passed between banks indefinitely. See [SBP's ePRC guidelines](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/documents/circulars/FEC5-Annex-A_%281%29.pdf).

A local payout does not automatically mean the wrong code. Elevate's supported routes below are a useful example of that.

## What happens with a USD transfer?

Take the same $2,000. With a supported USD wire, your Pakistani bank receives the foreign remittance intended for you. Correspondent banks can still be involved. Your bank can convert it to PKR, so using this route does not necessarily mean keeping a dollar balance.

Company / payment platform → USD international wire → your Pakistani bank → PKR conversion, where applicable

*The bank handling the foreign receipt can review the payment's purpose and supporting documents.*

For the first payment, expect questions about the sender, your work and the source of funds. Your branch or operations team may call or email you. Have your contract, invoices and platform payment records ready. Explain the services you provided and ask for the appropriate purpose code, including 9186 where it applies.

There's a useful update here. SBP's April 2026 reforms provide for a one-time declaration of services and tagging the relevant purpose code to the exporter's account. That should simplify recurring payments. A change in your work or a particular transaction may still need clarification. [SBP's announcement](https://www.sbp.org.pk/assets/documents/press-release/Pr-06-Apr-2026.pdf) sets out the changes.

**The trade-off is time and cost.** A wire can take a few business days end to end and may carry intermediary fees. The one-working-day bank-processing requirement announced by SBP is not a promise that the entire international journey takes one day. Deel also explains that [SWIFT intermediaries can charge additional fees](https://help.letsdeel.com/hc/en-gb/articles/14391381019537-About-Withdrawal-Fees-and-Withdrawal-Minimums).

The benefit is being able to clarify the paperwork with the bank handling your foreign receipt. Still, check the first PRC. Choosing USD alone does not guarantee 9186.

## What if your client is an individual?

An individual US client may just want to make a domestic bank transfer. They probably don't want to sign up for a corporate payroll platform to pay one invoice. A small company may feel the same way.

A service providing US receiving details can help. Your client pays those details, the funds appear in your balance, and you withdraw them to Pakistan. Elevate Pay offers this route to eligible users, including receiving dollars through ACH, the US bank-transfer network. Confirm that your account accepts the particular sender and payment type. Elevate explains [how to use its USD receiving details](https://www.elevatepay.co/blog/how-do-i-use-my-usd-account-details).

Wise and Revolut often come up here, but don't assume all three services are available on the same terms:

- **Wise** currently says it cannot provide USD account details to customers with an address in Pakistan. See its [USD account restrictions](https://wise.com/help/articles/2810318/can-i-get-usd-account-details).
- **Revolut** does not list Pakistan as a supported country of residence for opening a personal account. See its [supported countries](https://help.revolut.com/help/profile-and-plan/profile-plan/verifying-identity/what-countries-are-supported/).

Your client using a service abroad and you being eligible to hold an account with it are two separate things. Wise's PKR route uses local transfers and limits recipients to personal accounts, as described in its [PKR transfer guide](https://wise.com/help/articles/2932334/guide-to-pkr-transfers).

I couldn't verify a universal PRC outcome for Wise or Revolut. I wouldn't say every payment through either service produces 9471. Check the exact route before relying on it for your export documentation.

## Why I would look at Elevate Pay

Elevate publishes a [bank-by-bank PRC status page](https://www.elevatepay.co/prc-status). Its **Salary** flow currently lists 9186 as available for **NayaPay, Bank Alfalah, UBL, Soneri and Faysal Bank**. Faysal is listed as an IBFT payout. The **Home Remittance** flow lists 9471 for those destinations. At the time of checking, Meezan's Salary route also lists 9471.

Choosing Elevate alone isn't enough. Pick the appropriate flow and a supported destination. The page says the code is available, which is not an unconditional guarantee for every transaction. The app's Salary label also does not determine whether you are legally an employee.

For eligible freelance IT income, my preference would be to check that table and use Elevate's supported Salary route to NayaPay, Alfalah or UBL. Then I'd inspect the PRC before making it my regular route.

## Check NayaPay's limit before withdrawing

I had understood the monthly remittance limit to be Rs. 10 lakh. NayaPay's current help page lists **up to Rs. 15 lakh (Rs. 1,500,000)** in monthly foreign remittances for an upgraded wallet, with a lower initial limit. Check your own account's limit in the app. Other incoming and outgoing limits are separate. See [NayaPay's account limits](https://help.nayapay.com/article/68-are-there-any-account-limits).

NayaPay is an electronic money institution rather than a conventional bank. If your payments exceed your wallet's limit, consider a supported bank account, such as Alfalah or UBL, and confirm that account's own restrictions.

## What I would do before the next payment

1. Confirm whether the money is freelance IT export income, employment income or something else.
2. Ask the platform which currency and route it will use, and which institution supplies the PRC information.
3. Confirm account compatibility, fees and limits with the receiving bank or wallet.
4. Send an initial payment through the chosen route and inspect its PRC before using it every month.
5. Keep the PRC, invoice, contract and payment reference together. If the code is wrong, request a correction with those documents.

For me, the payment is only fully sorted when the paperwork matches the work.
